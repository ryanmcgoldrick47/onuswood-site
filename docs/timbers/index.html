The timbers

We work with nine Australian timbers. Each one behaves differently with light behind it, so we describe them plainly: the colour, the grain, where it grows and what it feels like in a room.

Why timber, and why these

Light changes timber. A shade that looks plain in daylight comes alive at night, when the grain is lit from behind and the thin edges glow.

Australian hardwoods are dense, stable and full of colour, from the near-white of Victorian ash to the deep red of jarrah. That range lets you match a light to a floor, a joinery finish or a mood.

Every product page shows which timbers that piece is made in, and the price changes with your choice. Rarer species cost a little more because they are harder to get in usable boards.

Why you'll see joins

Most lights carved from timber start as one big, perfect block, and most of that block ends up on the workshop floor. We do it the other way round.

We use as much usable timber from each tree as we can: shorter lengths, narrower boards and the offcuts from larger pieces. We join them, laminate them and turn them, and we leave the seams and glue lines visible rather than hiding them.

Those joins are part of the design. They show how a piece was made, they bring together the natural differences in colour and grain from board to board, and they mean far less of a precious resource is wasted.

Nine species

Swatches are a guide only. Real timber varies board to board, which is the point.

  • Tasmanian oak

    Colour
    Pale straw to light honey
    Grain
    Straight and even, with fine gum veins
    Grows
    Tasmania and Victoria's high country

    Bright, calm and modern. Glows golden when lit from behind.

    Used in 7 pieces.

  • Victorian ash

    Colour
    Pale cream to light pink-brown
    Grain
    Straight with a clear growth-ring figure
    Grows
    Cool mountain forests of Victoria

    Light and airy. Takes a soft matt finish beautifully.

    Used in 3 pieces.

  • Spotted gum

    Colour
    Light brown to deep chocolate
    Grain
    Wavy, with a ripple figure that catches the light
    Grows
    Coastal NSW and south-east Queensland

    Strong and lively. The figure shimmers as you walk past.

    Used in 10 pieces.

  • Blackbutt

    Colour
    Golden yellow to pale brown
    Grain
    Straight and moderately coarse
    Grows
    Coastal NSW and Queensland

    Honest and warm. A natural fit for coastal homes.

    Used in 3 pieces.

  • Jarrah

    Colour
    Rich red to deep burgundy
    Grain
    Interlocked, with occasional fiddleback
    Grows
    South-west Western Australia

    Dramatic and dense. Deepens to a dark red over time.

    Used in 6 pieces.

  • Tasmanian blackwood

    Colour
    Golden brown with darker bands
    Grain
    Straight to wavy, with chatoyant figure
    Grows
    North-west Tasmania's wet forests

    Our most prized species. Deep, lustrous and quietly luxurious.

    Used in 6 pieces.

  • Huon pine

    Colour
    Pale yellow to straw
    Grain
    Very fine and close, with bird's-eye flecks
    Grows
    South-west Tasmania (recovered timber only)

    Rare, fragrant and soft-gold. Used only for small, special pieces.

    Used in 2 pieces.

  • River red gum

    Colour
    Pink-red to deep red-brown
    Grain
    Interlocked and wavy
    Grows
    Along the Murray-Darling river systems

    Bold and characterful, full of figure and movement.

    Used in 2 pieces.

  • Recycled hardwood

    Colour
    Mixed browns with old nail marks
    Grain
    Varies board to board
    Grows
    Salvaged from old Australian buildings

    Every piece carries a history. No two lights match.

    Used in 5 pieces.
